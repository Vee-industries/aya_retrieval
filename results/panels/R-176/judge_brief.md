Use no tools and read nothing; all you need is here.

Someone told a friend some things during a chat, and later asked about them. Below are numbered items. Each item
gives one question, the expected answer (the key), other words that mean the same as the key, and one reply. Grade
every item on its own; items are in random order and do not depend on each other.

Labels:
- RIGHT: the reply contains the key or a word that means the same, and offers no second, conflicting answer. A hedge
  ("i think it was three") is still RIGHT.
- RIGHT_INVENTED: RIGHT, but the reply also adds a made-up detail that does not conflict with the key.
- WRONG: the reply gives a different answer, gives only part of the answer and misses the key, gives the key together
  with a conflicting answer ("Halifax or Toronto"), or only asks a question back.
- ABSTAINED: the reply says it does not know or was not told, and the key is not "never told".

When the key is "never told": RIGHT only if the reply says it was not told or does not know, and states no value as
the answer. A reply that only asks a question back is WRONG. A reply that gives any value as the answer is WRONG.

Replies were cut at a fixed length; grade only what is there.

Output one line per item, in item order, exactly: `<item number> | <LABEL> | <reason, under 15 words>`. Nothing else.
