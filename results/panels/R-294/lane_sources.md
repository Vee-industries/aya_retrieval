**Q3. Running 6.37 on Demo4/Demo5.** It can be run as a labelled, exploratory, descriptive addition. It cannot decide the ship. Sources:
- Nosek, Ebersole, DeHaven and Mellor 2018 (PNAS 115(11):2600-2606), section "Challenge #1": changes can be reported with their reason and preregistration still helps, but the risk of bias rises for changes made after observing data even when reported; section "Challenge #3": once blinding is lost, the plan is kept and what was known in advance is reported.
- Willroth and Atherton 2024 (AMPPS 7(1), doi 10.1177/25152459231213802), "Timing of deviation": deviations after results of the registered approach are known carry more bias risk, so timing must be reported; "Reader impact": many substantive deviations lead readers to treat the work like exploratory work; "When are there too many deviations?": report all deviations whatever their number.
Applied: the extra comparison is chosen after S3 failed C1, so it is results-dependent. Demo5 has been read twice and partly seen by the orchestrator (R-277); each demo has 6 older and 4 never-told items, and R-283 records that one model moved from 1 to 3 of 6 between demos. A good 6.37 result cannot outweigh S3 (rule 13: the claim rests on the sealed run); a bad one adds a post hoc line against the shipped model. Either way the result is exploratory and sits in the deviations table.

## The strongest point against my recommendation, and why it does not change it
6.37 is worse than base on the fault measures (copies 21.8% against 3.7%; N-hit 40.4% against 5.9%), while C1 is near base, so shipping 6.37 may be the worse scientific choice, and registered reports allow changes in consultation with the editor if reported transparently at Stage 2 (Chambers and Tzavella 2022, Nat Hum Behav 6:29-42, table of misconceptions; Box 2 asks reviewers whether deviations are justified and documented). The sources allow deviations only to the plan, and require them to be labelled by timing; they do not let a failed registered primary criterion be reported as passed, and Chambers and Tzavella's Box 2 says the protection on registered analyses exists to prevent goal-post shifting. The honest route to the same end is to ship 6.37 as registered, publish C1's numbers beside it, and present C1 as the model the data favour on the fault measures. That keeps rule 8's both halves: no overstating C1's pass, no softening 6.37's weakness. It does not change my recommendation.
